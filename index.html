Legible but Not Localized: Jailbreak Geometry in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models Is Attack-Specific

Yubin Im, Myeonghyun Kim, Seongmin Kim, Haehyun Cho

EMNLP 2026 · Proceedings of the 2026 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing

PDF ↗
Abstract

Activation-based jailbreak defenses rest on a fixed assumption: a successful jailbreak leaves an internal trace stable enough across attacks for one probe to catch new ones. We show it fails in mixture-of-experts language models. Within an attack, routing makes the jailbreak legible: router logits distinguish compliance from refusal, and a few experts carry the signal. But the signature does not survive across attacks.

A routing probe trained on three attacks performs at chance on a held-out fourth (pooled leave-one-attack-out balanced accuracy 0.52, 95 percent CI [0.50, 0.61]). Controls on two dense models show near-chance residual-stream transfer to held-out roleplay, so the failure is not specific to MoE routing. Nor is it an artifact of separable regions of activation space: the comply-versus-refuse direction itself shifts across attacks at every network depth.

One systematic trace remains in the compliance state’s effective dimensionality, but it is a signature of shape, not location, beyond directional probes. Across six open-weight MoE models and four jailbreak attacks, in routing and residual streams alike, the jailbroken state is legible and locally structured within each attack, yet its location is attack-specific. Reading activations alone cannot be assumed to catch a jailbreak the probe was not built for.

DryJIN: Detecting Information Leaks in Android Applications

Minseong Choi, Yubin Im, Steve Ko, Yonghwi Kwon, Yuseok Jeon, Haehyun Cho

IFIP SEC 2024 · 39th IFIP International Conference on ICT Systems Security and Privacy Protection

PDF ↗
Abstract

Android devices, handling sensitive data like call records and text messages, are prone to privacy breaches. Existing information flow tracking systems face difficulties in detecting these breaches due to two main challenges: the multi-layered Android platform using different programming languages (Java and C/C++), and the complex, event-driven execution flow of Android apps that complicates tracking, especially across these language barriers.

Our system, DryJIN, addresses this by effectively tracking information flow within and across both Java and native modules. Utilizing symbolic execution for native code data flows and integrating it with Java data flows, DryJIN enhances existing static analysis techniques (Argus-SAF, JuCify, and FlowDroid) to cover previously unaddressed information flow patterns.

We validated DryJIN’s effectiveness through a comprehensive evaluation on over 168k apps, including malware and real-world apps, demonstrating its superiority over current state-of-the-art methods.
